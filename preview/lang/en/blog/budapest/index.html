Budapest: Pearl of the Danube

Attractions, kosher and Michelin restaurants, practical information and tips for the Israeli traveler

Photo: Dirk Beyer / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0
Budapest is two cities joined by the Danube: hilly Buda, with the Castle District and the Fisherman's Bastion, and flat, lively Pest, with the Parliament, the cafés and the Jewish Quarter. It is a city of thermal baths, stunning architecture and rich Jewish history, and flying there from Tel Aviv takes about three and a half hours on a direct flight.

🏰 Top attractions

Photo: Dirk Beyer / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0
1

The Parliament Building

A neo-Gothic building constructed between 1885 and 1904. It has 691 rooms, and its dome rises to a height of 96 meters. Since 2000 it has displayed the Holy Crown of Hungary and the other coronation regalia.

💡 Tip: It looks best from the other side of the river, from the Fisherman's Bastion or from a Danube cruise.
Photo: Perituss / Wikimedia Commons · CC0
2

Fisherman's Bastion

Neo-Romanesque lookout terraces in Buda's Castle District, built between 1895 and 1902.

💡 Tip: From here you get one of the most impressive views of the Parliament and the Danube.
Photo: Jakub Hałun / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0
3

The Castle District and Buda Castle

Here you will find the Royal Palace, the National Gallery, Matthias Church and the funicular.

💡 Tip: Combine the visit on the same day with the Fisherman's Bastion – they are right next to each other.
Photo: Marc Ryckaert / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY 3.0
4

Széchenyi Baths

One of the largest spa complexes in Europe, in the City Park. The water is drawn from a depth of 1,250 meters, and the pools are cooled to 30 to 40 degrees.

💡 Tip: Bring flip-flops and a swimsuit.
Photo: Acediscovery / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY 4.0
5

The Dohány Street Synagogue

The largest synagogue in Europe. The complex also includes a Jewish museum and the "Tree of Life" Holocaust memorial.

💡 Tip: You can join Budapest Info's Jewish Quarter tour, which also passes the Orthodox synagogue on Kazinczy Street.
Photo: Nikodem Nijaki / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0
6

The Shoes on the Danube Bank

A Holocaust memorial by Gyula Pauer, on the Danube promenade, near the Parliament.

💡 Tip: It is within walking distance of the Parliament, so it is easy to combine both on the same route.
Photo: Wilfredor / Wikimedia Commons · CC0
7

The Chain Bridge (Széchenyi Lánchíd)

The bridge connecting Buda and Pest; at its Pest end stands the Gresham Palace.

💡 Tip: It is especially stunning in the evening, when it is lit up.
Photo: Carlos Delgado / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0
8

St. Stephen's Basilica and Andrássy Avenue

The largest basilica in the city. Along the avenue you can see the Opera House and the "House of Terror", and it leads all the way to Heroes' Square.

💡 Tip: A good walking route: from the basilica, along the avenue, to Heroes' Square and the City Park (where Széchenyi is).
Photo: P. Hughes / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY 4.0
★

Bonus: The Great Market Hall

Next to the Liberty Bridge.

🕒 Closed on Sundays and holidays; on Saturdays closes at 15:00 (piaconline.hu).

🧭 Practical information

When to visit

The most pleasant seasons are April–May and September–October: mild weather and fewer tourists.

Getting around

Bus line 100E connects the airport to Deák Ferenc tér, runs around the clock and the ride takes about 40 minutes. A ticket for the line: 2,500 forints, and you can pay by credit card directly on the bus. A 24-hour travel pass: 2,750 forints; 72 hours: 5,750 forints (neither is valid on line 100E). Tickets are bought in the BudapestGO app.

Currency

In Hungary you pay in Hungarian forints (HUF / Ft), not euros. Credit cards are accepted almost everywhere, but it is worth keeping some cash for markets and stalls.

🇮🇱 Tips for the Israeli traveler

  1. A direct flight from Tel Aviv to Budapest takes about 3 hours and 35 minutes. The route is flown by Wizz Air, El Al, Arkia and Israir.
  2. Chabad House is at Károly körút 20. It offers prayers and Shabbat meals for travelers; advance registration is required.
  3. Most kosher food is concentrated in the Jewish Quarter (District VII), around Kazinczy, Dob and Wesselényi streets.
  4. The Dohány synagogue is closed to visits on Shabbat, so plan it for other days.
  5. On Shabbat it is worth booking a meal in advance, at Chabad or at Carmel.
